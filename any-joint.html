What the seven have in common

Every joint lives in one canonical block: the section runs x, z ∈ [−0.5, 0.5] and the beam runs y ∈ [0, 3]. Six oriented half-spaces are declared over that block, each one a predicate n · p ≥ d. Predicates are intersected inside a group, groups are unioned. Material satisfying the rule is removed and replaced. Its complement is the beam that stays.

Slots may collapse onto the same support plane, which is how the rule degenerates cleanly. SJ5 is the case where all six half-spaces coincide and the whole program reduces to a single saw cut.

Why a catalogue is a sampling artefact

A catalogue entry is a fixed tuple of plane coefficients. Nothing in the representation privileges those particular tuples. Move the coefficients continuously and the joint moves with them, passing through geometries that are perfectly buildable and carry no name.

The traverse walks the closed loop SJ1 → SJ2 → SJ3 → SJ4 → SJ5 → SJ6 → SJ7 → SJ1. Watch the rule while it runs. When a half-space slides off the block its predicate becomes trivially true and drops out of the expression, and when two slots meet on one support plane the expression collapses further. That is how one topology becomes another with no discontinuity anywhere.
